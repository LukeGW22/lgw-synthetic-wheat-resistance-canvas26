Greenbug and Hessian fly resistance in Texas A&M breeding lines

The poster covers greenbug and Hessian fly screening and GWAS in synthetic HRW and the SDL298 synthetic-derived lines. This page follows that resistance into the Texas A&M breeding lines: where their resistance maps, which signals hold across models, what each locus does, and how loci stack. Breeding lines are the 2024–2026 TAMU advanced lines.

4Which signals hold across models

Each trait was fitted with six kinship-based GAPIT models (MLM, CMLM, MLMM, SUPER, FarmCPU, BLINK) on two phenotype scales (glmer % and empirical logit), 12 fits in all. Each square is one fit where the signal passed Bonferroni 0.05. A signal found in most fits does not depend on the model or the scale.

5What each locus does in the breeding lines

Mean resistance of lines homozygous for the resistance allele at the GWAS lead SNP, against lines with the other allele. Under each locus: how many genotyped lines carry the resistance allele.

Resistance alleleOther allele

The loci were found in these same lines, so these effects are upper estimates. The greenbug signals on 3A and 4A pass the GWAS threshold but move mean resistance by only 1 to 2 points.

9Breeding lines vs SDL298, locus by locus

Methods. Greenhouse seedling screens, 2023–2026; R % converted to the empirical logit; line BLUEs from a mixed model with genotype fixed and screen year random. About 61K SNPs; GAPIT models as above.
